Organization
NC State University / Formula SAE
Recognition

Problem
Our Formula SAE team needed to compare finite element analysis (FEA) predictions for the chassis against physical strain-gauge measurements.
Role
I designed and fabricated a custom chassis test rig, and oversaw development of a PCB-amplifier system that collected and logged strain-gauge data from the vehicle chassis during on-track testing.
Process
I analyzed the simulation and test results to evaluate how accurate the model was.

About me

NC State · Class of 2026

I’m drawn to work where ideas become useful things you can build, test, and improve. That curiosity took me from high-school robotics to mechanical engineering at NC State, where I paired my engineering studies with a minor in Forest Management. I enjoy solving hands-on problems with a team, especially in robotics, electric vehicles, and hardware development.

My interest in engineering goes hand in hand with caring about the places and resources our work affects. Spending a summer on conservation and trail projects in Colorado gave me a direct appreciation for the effort it takes to care for public lands. I want to keep that perspective in my engineering work: make thoughtful design choices, use materials and energy responsibly, and build solutions that are useful over the long term.

Notes: what has shaped my approach

  1. Learning by building

    At NC State, I studied mechanical engineering and completed a minor in Forest Management. I’ve found that I learn best by moving between analysis and hands-on work—asking questions, trying an approach, and using what happens to make the next version better.

  2. Time in the field

    Rocky Mountain Conservancy · Boulder, Colorado · Summer 2023

    With AmeriCorps, I joined National Park Service and U.S. Forest Service crews on conservation and trail work. Weeks in the backcountry—planning routes, improving drainage, restoring trails, and working with specialized tools—taught me to respect the environment, work carefully with limited resources, and rely on the people around me.

  3. Staying connected to student engineering

    After competing in Formula SAE, I returned as an alumni structural design reviewer. It’s rewarding to help student teams think critically about their designs, engineering evidence, and safety.

  4. Where it started

    FIRST Robotics was my first experience turning a rough idea into a working machine. I spent four years with Team SPORK #3196, taking on mechanical, project-planning, and driving roles. That mix of building and teamwork is still a big part of what I enjoy about engineering.

  5. Thinking beyond the design

    Through NC State’s Albright Entrepreneurs Village, I’ve also explored how engineering ideas move toward real products. It’s helped me think about not only whether something works, but who it serves and how it can be made practical.
